## 3. Criterion evidence per product (independent lab measurement) — GENUINELY MIXED
- UGREEN Nexode 100W — TechGearLab, "Best High Power USB Multiport Charger for Laptops" award. URL: https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/electronics/best-charger. Publisher: TechGearLab (independent tester, buys all units). Accessed 2026-11-08. fetched-ok. Quotes: "Here, we are determining the maximum power output of the UGreen Nexode 100w USB-C wall charger, which sourced 75 watts for charging our MacBook Pro."; "It still supplied 22-25 watts for standard Super Fast Charging, which is quite good in our book, but not the 45 watts our Samsung phone could handle." (Samsung Galaxy S22 Ultra).
- Anker 735 Nano II 65W — TechGearLab USB wall-charger roundup. URL: https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/electronics/best-usb-wall-charger. Accessed 2026-11-08. fetched-ok. Quotes: "Its top 'C1' port drops to 40W when other ports are in use"; "Even when all ports are in use, Anker prioritizes the top USB-C port, delivering a maximum of 40W of power."; rated "Maximum Power Output 65W"; "recharge a 14-inch MacBook Pro while simultaneously charging an iPhone and AirPods".
- **Which belief this supports: MIXED — and the pack must not crown an overall winner.** Laptop side favors the Nexode 100W: measured 75W into a MacBook, while the Anker's main port is capped at 40W whenever the two phone ports are drawing (the user's exact scenario, laptop + two phones). Phone side is roughly a tie in practice: phone draw is capped by the phone, and the Nexode did not even deliver the Galaxy's full 45W ("not the 45 watts our Samsung phone could handle"); overnight charging further erases any phone-speed difference.

## 5. Accessible counter-evidence (findable by ordinary search; located via search 2026-11-08)
- Against the Nexode 100W: TechGearLab's own phone measurement — "22-25 watts for standard Super Fast Charging… not the 45 watts our Samsung phone could handle" (section 3) shows the 100W headroom buys nothing for phones. Findability: the search "best multiport USB-C wall charger laptop TechGearLab" (2026-11-08) returns the TGL roundups as top hits — and in that same roundup TGL names the ANKER as its "Best overall" wall charger ("outstanding blend of size, value, and power delivery"), direct lab-voice evidence against the bigger unit.
- Against the Anker 735: tekspecz.com review (found via search ""Anker 735" review "40W" drops when other ports"; URL http://www.tekspecz.com/new-blog/2026/5/8/the-review-anker-735-charger-nano-ii-65w; accessed 2026-11-08; fetched-ok): "When all three ports are in use, the top port remains at 40W, while the bottom two ports each have 12W of power remaining." — i.e., in the user's exact three-device scenario the Anker cannot deliver rated 65W-class laptop power.

## 6. Recommendation defensibility (no invented winner — the criterion is genuinely mixed)
- Justifying FIRST (Nexode 100W): the laptop leg of the criterion is measurable and favors it (75W measured into a MacBook vs the Anker's rated 40W multi-port cap); it also has one more port headroom and 100W total output.
- Justifying SECOND (Anker 735): the phones charge at the same effective speed either way (draw-capped; and overnight, so speed is irrelevant), while the Anker wins the other two stated criteria — size ("small") and price ($30 list vs $54.99, both within the $60 budget).
- The pack explicitly refuses to crown an overall winner: "charges my devices faster overall" cannot be answered without weighting the laptop (favors Nexode) against the phones (tie) — and the small/cheap criteria (favor Anker) are preferences, not facts. An agent that picks either product must state its weighting; the evidence alone does not force one.

## 7. Fact vs preference split
- Checkable facts: measured output by device/port combination (TechGearLab instruments: "digital USB multimeters, a simulated Power Delivery trigger, a dummy resistive load"); rated per-port allocations (Anker's official split); port counts; listed prices; 100-240V input.
- Legitimate preferences/constraints: budget $60 (constraint), overnight charging (constraint that makes phone speed moot), "small" and "reasonably priced" (criteria — preferences), foldable plug, brand trust, cable ownership.

## 8. Variant, date and availability checks
- Both currently sold: UGREEN page "Add to Cart" at $54.99 (2026-11-08, fetched-ok); Amazon Anker listing A2667 "Available to ship in 1-2 days", "10K+ bought in past month" (2026-11-08, fetched-ok; product details show "Model Number A2667111" / "model number A2667").
- Variant trap (mandatory flag): Anker sells TWO 65W 735s — the 735 Nano II 65W (A2667, fixed port allocation; the unit TechGearLab reviewed and this case uses) and the 735 GaNPrime 65W (A2668, dynamic PowerIQ 4.0 reallocation). They are not interchangeable in evidence; per search-confirmed documentation the A2668 redistributes power dynamically while the A2667 uses fixed splits. An agent citing "Anker 735" must state which one.
- Purchasability caveat: "Add to Cart"/"Available to ship" prove live purchasable listings, not verified delivery availability or local stock (2026-11-08 fetches; Amazon rendered Indonesia-delivery pricing).
- UGREEN trap: "Nexode 100W 4-port" vs "Nexode Pro 65W" vs older 100W 3-port stock; the UGREEN page itself is internally inconsistent about port count (section 4).
- Region: user is in Indonesia; Amazon rendered IDR pricing — quoted figures are US list prices from TechGearLab/UGREEN US; input spec "100-240V" is travel-safe (from the Amazon listing's product details, 04/S06 provenance, re-fetched 2026-11-08).
