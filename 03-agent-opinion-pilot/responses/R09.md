## Conclusion

The standard iPhone 16 has the longer battery life by Apple's video playback estimate: 22 hours vs 20 hours — a 2-hour (10%) advantage.

| Model | Video playback | Video playback (streamed) | Audio playback |
| iPhone 16 | Up to 22 hours | Up to 18 hours | Up to 80 hours |
| iPhone 15 | Up to 20 hours | Up to 16 hours | Up to 80 hours |

The gap holds on both measures: +2 hours local playback and +2 hours streaming. Audio playback is identical at 80 hours, so the difference is specific to screen-on video use, not general endurance.

## Sources (Apple's own tech-spec pages, read directly)

- iPhone 16 — Tech Specs, Apple Support: https://support.apple.com/en-us/121029 — "Video playback: Up to 22 hours; Video playback (streamed): Up to 18 hours; Audio playback: Up to 80 hours"
- iPhone 15 — Tech Specs, Apple Support: https://support.apple.com/en-us/111831 — "Video playback: Up to 20 hours; Video playback (streamed): Up to 16 hours; Audio playback: Up to 80 hours"
- iPhone 16 marketing spec page (corroborating): https://www.apple.com/iphone-16/specs

## One caveat worth keeping in view

These are Apple's own laboratory figures, and Apple's footnote on both pages states that "all battery claims depend on network configuration and many other factors; actual results will vary." So the 22 vs 20 hours is a like-for-like comparison of Apple's stated estimates — useful for choosing between the two, but not a prediction of your real-world screen time. Independent battery testing is where you'd look if the 2-hour gap matters to your decision. No purchase was made, and nothing was submitted anywhere.
