Your belief is correct: the OLED Model has twice the built-in storage — 64 GB versus 32 GB. I checked Nintendo's own specification pages rather than retailer listings.

| Model | Built-in internal storage (official wording) |
| Nintendo Switch – OLED Model | "Storage: 64 GB" (UK page: "System memory: 64 GB") |
| Nintendo Switch console (HAC-001(-01)) | "32 GB of internal storage, a portion of which is reserved for use by the system" |
| (Nintendo Switch Lite — excluded per your brief) | 32 GB |

Both figures already exclude microSD cards. Nintendo lists expandable storage separately on the same spec sheets ("microSDHC or microSDXC cards up to 2TB, sold separately"), so the 64/32 GB numbers are the internal memory only. One caveat worth carrying into your decision: these are nominal capacities. Nintendo states on both models that a portion of internal memory is reserved for system use, so the space you can actually fill with games is smaller than 64 GB and 32 GB respectively. Nintendo does not publish exact usable figures on these pages; third-party outlets report roughly 57 GB usable on the OLED and roughly 26 GB on the standard model, but treat those as unofficial estimates, not Nintendo statements. The difference is worth noting for a practical reason: the 2× internal storage is one of the few OLED advantages besides the screen, the adjustable stand, the enhanced audio and the dock's wired LAN port. If you play mostly downloaded titles, the standard Switch's 32 GB fills very quickly and effectively assumes a microSD purchase.
